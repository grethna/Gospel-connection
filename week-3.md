# A Lesson in Faith and Support

Dear posterity,

I want to tell you about a time when I questioned whether the career I had chosen was right for me. 
I was struggling to organize my time for studying and homework, and I began to feel anxious. 
I withdrew from some activities and did not finish every assignment. 
I tried to care for myself by going to bed earlier and changing my schedule so I could wake up earlier, 
rest my mind, and regain energy. During this difficult time, I prayed and went to the temple.

At the temple, the person whose ordinances we were performing was named Angel.
A temple worker told me that I looked like an angel. That kind comment touched my heart. 
I felt that God sends angels to us on earth through people who offer support and comfort. 
The experience reminded me that I was not alone in facing my worries.

After this experience, I became more intentional about caring for my emotional and physical health.
I tried not to skip meals or stay up late. I also learned to respect myself more, appreciate my strengths, 
and be compassionate toward my weaknesses.

This experience also changed how I treat other people. During a visit to Adobe, 
I found myself giving more compliments to people I met. I decided to do the same for my classmates, roommates, and parents. 
I want to pray with them and help by listening and asking thoughtful questions. 
I have also begun to see that difficulty can teach us as we learn, take risks, and do our best. 
I want to greet and treat people with kindness, 
remembering President Eyring’s example of seeing each person as someone who may be facing a serious challenge.

I see a connection between my experience and transformation in an ETL pipeline. 
In ETL, information is gathered, transformed into a more useful form, and then put to use. 
My experience gave me signs that something needed to change: 
I felt anxious, struggled with my schedule, and sometimes neglected sleep and meals. 
Through prayer, attending the temple, and changing my routine, I began to respond differently. 
The result was not that every challenge disappeared; I became more intentional about caring for myself and showing kindness to others. 
The ETL idea helps me describe that process of taking what I learned and putting it into practice.

When I felt anxious and unsure about my career, I chose to pray and attend the temple. 
I asked Heavenly Father for help, and I also made practical changes to my routine by trying to sleep earlier and wake up earlier. 
This experience taught me that I can seek spiritual help while also taking steps to care for myself.

This experience did not make every challenge disappear, and I am still learning. 
It helped prepare me to face difficulty with more care for my health, more respect for my own strengths, 
and more compassion for my weaknesses. 
I want to become a stronger and more durable warrior for the Gospel of Jesus Christ by continuing to turn to God, 
making wise changes, and serving people with kindness. 
To me, strength in the Gospel includes moving forward faithfully even when I feel uncertain.

I believe this experience matters because it taught me to take my well-being seriously and to be patient with myself while I learn. 
If you face a time when you question your path, I hope you will remember that you can seek God’s help, make thoughtful changes, and take one step at a time. 
Feeling anxious or struggling with a task does not mean you have no strengths. 
Be compassionate with yourself, and look for the people God may send to support you.

Isaiah 49:2 describes the Lord’s servant as a polished shaft held in His quiver. 
President Henry B. Eyring teaches that trials can polish us as we turn to the Savior and endure them well. 
I connect those ideas to my experience: I did not have to solve everything at once, but I could keep praying, care for my health, and accept support. 
President Eyring also shares advice to treat people as if they may be facing serious trouble. 
That reminds me why I want to compliment people, listen, and show kindness: 
I may not know the challenges they are carrying. (Isaiah 49:2)
